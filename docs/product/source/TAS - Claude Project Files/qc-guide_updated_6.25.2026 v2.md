# QUALITY CONTROL GUIDE

## PURPOSE
When /qc-check is called, perform a structured review of
the full workbook and produce a prioritized list of flags
and observations for the director. The director makes the
final call on every item. Do not render a go or no-go
decision. Surface everything material and let the director
decide what to act on.

---

## REVIEW SEQUENCE
Work through the following in order.

---

### 1. PROCEDURAL DOCUMENTATION CHECK

Review the Tax Reconciliation tab and the Bank Statement
Review tab.

For each tab confirm:
- The procedure was completed and the tab is populated
- For the Bank Statement Review tab, focus on whether
  at least one full period (typically the trailing
  twelve months) has been reconciled and documented.
  Individual monthly variances do not need to be
  flagged. Only flag a full-period reconciliation gap
  if it is material and undocumented. If a director
  note explains why a reconciliation was not completed
  for a given period, that is sufficient.
- Any unreconciled variances at the full-period level
  are documented with an explanation. The materiality
  threshold for tax reconciliation variances is 5% of
  the recalculated SDE for that period. Variances below
  5% of SDE are acceptable without requiring narrative
  revision, though they should still carry a brief
  explanation. Only flag an unreconciled variance if it
  exceeds 5% of SDE for the relevant period and is
  undocumented.
- The summary narrative on the tab accurately describes
  what the procedure found at the full-period level.
  Do not flag minor month-level discrepancies in the
  activity review. Flag only if the summary materially
  misstates or omits what is shown in the underlying
  tab.

Flag format:
"Tax Reconciliation: [specific issue or confirmation
that documentation is adequate]"
"Bank Statement Review: [specific issue or confirmation
that documentation is adequate]"

---

### 2. P&L REVIEW FOR UNUSUAL ITEMS AND MISSED ADDBACKS

Review the P&L tab across all periods looking for:
- Accounts or line items that appear unusual or
  inconsistent with the business type
- Items that may represent personal expenses, owner
  benefits, one-time costs, or non-recurring items
  that have not been added back on the SDE or Adjusted
  EBITDA tab
- Common items that are frequently missed: charitable
  contributions, meals and entertainment above a
  reasonable business level, auto expenses with personal
  use, insurance policies that benefit the owner
  personally, legal or professional fees that are
  non-recurring, rent paid to a related party

For each potential missed addback:
- Check whether it is already on the SDE or Adjusted
  EBITDA tab
- Check whether it was asked about in Q&A and
  appropriately resolved
- If it is not added back and not addressed in Q&A,
  flag it for the director

Flag format:
"Potential missed addback: [account name], [amount and
period]. This does not appear on the SDE tab and was
not addressed in Q&A. Director should confirm whether
this warrants an addback."

If an item was asked about in Q&A and the decision was
made not to add it back, no flag is needed.

---

### 3. SDE OR ADJUSTED EBITDA ACCURACY CHECK

Use the SDE Narrative Guide as the quality benchmark
for this section. The narrative should reflect the
analytical standard described there, including the
normalized expense base framework and the TTM
commentary approach.

Review the Recalculated SDE or Adjusted EBITDA tab
and confirm:

Math and mechanics:
- The net income figure ties to the P&L for each period
- Each addback is supported by documentation or Q&A
  commentary
- The calculated SDE or Adjusted EBITDA ties to the
  figures used in the narrative and executive summary

Addback commentary quality:
- The written commentary defending each addback is
  professional, clearly worded, and free of spelling
  errors
- The commentary accurately describes what the addback
  is and why it is being normalized
- The dollar amount in the commentary matches the
  dollar amount in the calculation
- The context and reasoning provided is defensible
  and would hold up to scrutiny from a buyer or
  their advisor

Narrative and addback defensibility check:
- For each addback on the SDE tab, confirm the defense
  provided is supported by a specific source in the
  workbook — a Q&A response, a P&L line item, a
  balance sheet entry, or a procedural tab. If no
  source can be identified, flag it.
- For each claim made in the working capital, SDE,
  and risks and opportunities narratives, confirm the
  underlying figure or fact ties to something visible
  in the workbook.
- Do not assume a narrative claim is correct because
  it reads as reasonable. If it cannot be verified
  against the workbook, flag it for the director to
  confirm before the report is finalized.

Narrative sanity check:
- The revenue and margin narrative explains changes
  using operational drivers visible in the P&L, not
  generic or vague language
- Addback items are not cited as drivers of earnings
  movement
- TTM commentary is framed as current YTD versus
  prior year same YTD, not TTM versus full prior year
  in absolute terms
- If the narrative says something increased or
  decreased, confirm that movement is visible in
  the underlying financials
- Flag any narrative language that does not foot to
  the numbers or reads as inconsistent with what
  the workbook shows
- Missing commentary is flagged inline per the
  standard in the SDE Narrative Guide

---

### 4. BALANCE SHEET REVIEW

Focus on the current reporting period. Stale balances
that have been consistent throughout the entire review
period are not a concern. Flag items that:
- Appear or change materially within the reporting
  periods being reviewed
- Could affect the SDE calculation
- Could affect the working capital conversation and
  are not reflected in the working capital narrative

Do not flag related party debt items, owner loans, or
other balance sheet debt accounts. These are legal and
transaction-level items that will be resolved through
the purchase agreement and are outside the scope of
the QoE review.

Flag format:
"Balance Sheet: [account name] shows [description of
issue]. Director should confirm whether this requires
documentation."

---

### 5. WORKING CAPITAL REVIEW

Use the Working Capital Guide as the quality benchmark
for this section. The narrative and calculation should
reflect the standards described there, including
component selection, math display format, and the
standard closing disclosure.

Review the Working Capital tab, Balance Sheet tab, and
working capital narrative on the Executive Summary tab
and confirm:
- The narrative aligns with the figures on the
  Working Capital tab
- The components included are appropriate for the
  business type and consistent with the Working
  Capital Guide
- Any material balance sheet items relevant to working
  capital are not missing from the narrative
- The math display in the summary is present and correct
- The standard closing disclosure is included verbatim
- Any items toggled off on the Working Capital tab
  that appear they should be included are flagged
- Omitted items that should be present given the
  business type are identified per the Working
  Capital Guide guidance
- Each figure cited in the working capital narrative
  ties to a specific cell or balance in the Working
  Capital tab or Balance Sheet tab. If a figure
  cannot be sourced, flag it.

Also consider whether any working capital related risks
or opportunities have not been captured in the risks
and opportunities section.

---

### 6. RISKS AND OPPORTUNITIES REVIEW

Use the Risks and Opportunities Guide as the quality
benchmark for this section. Items should reflect the
consolidation, materiality, and format standards
described there.

Review the risks and opportunities documented in the
workbook and consider:
- Whether material risks visible in the P&L, balance
  sheet, or Q&A are not documented
- Whether the consolidation is appropriate — no single
  theme should appear as multiple separate items
- Whether opportunities identified during the engagement
  are not captured
- For any area where the company was unable to provide
  commentary, confirm that the risk is documented
  clearly and that the report language is defensible.
  If the company could not explain something material,
  the report should say so explicitly rather than
  leaving a gap.
- Each risk or opportunity should be traceable to a
  specific source in the workbook. If an item cannot
  be sourced, flag it for the director to verify.

Note on formatting: risks and opportunities items are
standardly formatted with a leading dash as a bullet
marker. Do not flag this as a formatting error — it is
the firm's standard presentation format for this section.

Flag format:
"Potential undocumented risk: [description]. This does
not appear in the current risks and opportunities
section. Director should consider whether this warrants
inclusion."

---

### 7. NARRATIVE AND FORMATTING REVIEW

Review all written narrative sections for:
- Placeholder text that was not replaced (brackets,
  TBD, or template language left in the report)
- Company name or period references that are incorrect
  or inconsistent
- Narrative sections that appear blank or incomplete
- Numbers referenced in narratives that do not match
  the workbook
- Spelling errors or clearly unprofessional language
  in any narrative section

Do not flag minor formatting preferences, decimal
placement, or date format variations. Flag only
blatant errors that would be visible and embarrassing
in a final report.

---

## OUTPUT FORMAT

### Section-by-Section Output
Present each section with a status label first, followed
by brief supporting detail only if needed. The director
should be able to scan the status labels and move on
without reading further when a section is clean.

Status labels:
- NO ISSUES — section reviewed, nothing to flag
- FLAG — one or more items require director attention

Format for each section:

**[Section Name] — NO ISSUES**
[One sentence confirming what was checked, optional.
Keep it to a single line. Omit entirely if the status
label is self-explanatory.]

**[Section Name] — FLAG**
[Specific flag text. One flag per line or short
paragraph. Be direct and brief. No preamble.]

### Section Order
1. Procedural Documentation
2. P&L and Addback Review
3. SDE Accuracy and Narrative
4. Balance Sheet
5. Working Capital
6. Risks and Opportunities
7. Narrative and Formatting

### Director Action Summary
After all sections, include a single consolidated list
of every item requiring director follow-up. Label it:

**Items Requiring Director Follow-Up**

List each item as a one-line entry. This is a
scan-and-assign list, not a re-explanation. If there
are no flags across all sections, state:
"No items requiring follow-up."

---

## FILE-SPECIFIC RULES
- Surface issues and let the director decide. Do not
  render a go or no-go decision.
- Only flag items that are material. The threshold for
  quantitative flags is approximately 1% of the lowest
  SDE or Adjusted EBITDA year in the review period.
  Items below this threshold should be filtered out
  unless they indicate fraud risk, legal exposure, or
  a pattern of misstatement where the dollar amount
  alone understates the concern. For example, a small
  negative credit card balance may warrant a flag if
  it suggests a larger population of missing expenses
  even though the balance itself is immaterial.
- Tax reconciliation variances are held to a separate,
  higher threshold of 5% of recalculated SDE for the
  relevant period. Do not flag a tax-to-book variance
  or require narrative revision solely because the
  variance exceeds the standard 1% threshold, provided
  it remains below 5% of SDE.
- For all other quantitative flags (potential missed
  addbacks, balance sheet items, working capital
  components), apply the 1% threshold described above
  consistently across all sections.
- Qualitative flags (placeholder text, formatting
  errors, incorrect entity references, narrative
  inconsistencies) are surfaced regardless of dollar
  size as they represent report integrity issues
  independent of materiality.
- Stale balance sheet items that have been consistent
  throughout the entire review period are not a concern.
  Focus on items that change or appear within the
  reporting periods being analyzed.
- When the company was unable to provide commentary
  on a material item, flag whether the current
  documentation is sufficient to protect the firm.
- The SDE Narrative Guide, Working Capital Guide, and
  Risks and Opportunities Guide define the quality
  standard for their respective sections. Use them
  as the benchmark when assessing whether each section
  meets the firm's output standard.
- Do not assume any narrative claim is correct because
  it reads as reasonable. Every material claim should
  be traceable to a specific source in the workbook.
  Flag anything that cannot be verified.
- Before beginning the QC review, discard all figures,
  calculations, and observations derived from any prior
  version of the workbook in this conversation. Every
  number cited in a flag must be sourced from the
  current workbook only.
