# RISKS & OPPORTUNITIES GUIDE

## PURPOSE
When /risks-opportunities is called, analyze the full workbook and produce a prioritized list of opportunities and risks for the director to review. Present opportunities first, risks second. The director makes the final call on what goes in the workbook.

---

## SOURCES TO REVIEW
- Executive Summary tab (industry, company background)
- P&L tab (revenue trends, expense patterns, margins)
- Balance Sheet tab (unusual accounts, stale balances)
- Tax Reconciliation tab (variances, unreconciled items)
- Bank Statement Review tab (reconciling items, variances)
- Q&A tab (closed items and company responses only — open items are not themselves a risk, but use any relevant information from them to support other items)

---

## ANALYSIS PROCESS
1. Generate a full internal list of every potential opportunity and risk across all sources.
2. Consolidate into themes. Do not list the same issue multiple times in different forms. Examples:
   - Multiple bookkeeping errors → one financial reporting reliability item
   - Several large customers → one customer concentration item, not one per customer
3. Apply a materiality filter. Only surface items that could materially influence a buyer's decision or affect deal value by approximately 5% or more. Use judgment — err on the side of inclusion if uncertain.
4. Present opportunities first, then risks. No maximum — present what is material. Five items is perfectly fine. Twenty is the ceiling.

---

## FORMAT FOR DIRECTOR RESPONSE
Present results to the director with two clear headers:

**Opportunities**
[list each opportunity individually]

**Risks**
[list each risk individually]

These headers are for the director's review only and are not intended to be pasted into the workbook. Each item should stand alone as a clean paragraph ready to copy-paste directly into the workbook without the header.

---

## FORMAT FOR EACH ITEM
Each item should be:
- 2-4 sentences, concise but complete
- Specific — use actual numbers, account names, and year references where relevant
- Referenced to its source using Q&A numbers (Q9), tab names, or both
- Written in third person referring to "the company" and "the client"
- Explicit about what the risk or opportunity actually is
- Where the client can take action to mitigate a risk or realize an opportunity, call that out clearly without implying the risk is fully resolved

### Nonreliance Language
This is a quality of earnings engagement conducted under a nonreliance letter. Never phrase any item, in either section, as advising the client or director to "rely on" this workbook, its recalculated figures, its narratives, or its analysis, including in comparison to the CIM or any other source material. Where a figure or analysis is better supported than another source, describe it as such (for example, "supported by a transaction level review of the general ledger") without characterizing it as something the client or director may rely upon. Where independent verification is warranted, direct the client to confirm the item with the company or through their own due diligence rather than to rely on the figures presented here.

### Guidance for Risks
Be direct about the nature of the risk. Do not soften material findings. Frame it as something the client should be aware of and may want to investigate further prior to or after close. If there is a reasonable mitigation path available to the client, include it.

### Guidance for Opportunities
Be specific about what the opportunity is and what action would be required to realize it. Ground it in actual data from the workbook where possible.

---

## CONSOLIDATION BUCKETS
Think in themes, not line items:
- Bookkeeping quality / financial reporting reliability
- Owner dependency / key person risk
- Customer concentration / revenue quality
- Seasonal or weather-related revenue variability
- Expense management and job costing practices
- Balance sheet items requiring post-close attention
- Industry or market-specific risks

---

## EXAMPLE ITEM

The company generated approximately $478K, or ~19% of total FY25 revenue, from a single customer related to a one-time wedding venue project (Q9). Project-based revenue of this size is inherently non-recurring, and the client should not underwrite similar engagements in future periods without direct confirmation from the company on its current project pipeline. The more predictable maintenance revenue stream provides a stable earnings base and should be weighted more heavily when evaluating normalized earnings.

---

## DO NOT
- List the same risk multiple times in different forms
- Write a separate item for every individual account or line item issue
- Treat open Q&A items as standalone risks
- Present more than 20 items
- Refer to "the seller" — always use "the company"
- Add labels like "Risk:" or "Opportunity:" before each item — the section header handles that
- Soften or hedge material findings to avoid awkwardness
- Phrase any item as telling the client or director to "rely on" this workbook, its figures, or its analysis, whether standalone or in comparison to the CIM or other source material — this is a nonreliance engagement
- Write a risk item about the CIM containing errors, inconsistencies, or unsupported figures. The CIM is a preliminary marketing document, not a deliverable this firm stands behind, and discrepancies between the CIM and the recalculated workbook are expected and not, on their own, a risk worth flagging to the client. If a CIM discrepancy is being used to illustrate a genuine underlying issue (for example, a bookkeeping error or an addback that does not hold up), frame the item around that underlying issue and its support in the current workbook, not around the CIM being wrong
