# WORKING CAPITAL GUIDE

## PURPOSE
When /working-capital is called, produce a working capital
narrative with three sections. Output should be copy-paste
ready for the director.

---

## PRE-WRITING REVIEW
Before writing, review the following in order:

1. Working Capital tab — should be populated by the reviewer.
   If unpopulated, has errors, or does not reconcile to the
   balance sheet, flag to the director before proceeding. Note
   any accounts toggled "No" that appear they should be "Yes"
   and flag for the director.

2. Balance Sheet tab — review all current asset and current
   liability line items. Note anything unusual, stale, or
   inconsistent with operations.

3. AR Aging Report — if an AR aging schedule is present in the
   workbook, review it before drafting the AR component. Identify
   any balances that have been confirmed as uncollectible or
   written off based on available Q&A commentary. Remove confirmed
   bad debt from the AR figure used in the narrative and the math
   display, and disclose the adjustment explicitly in the AR
   paragraph. Do not assume any balance is bad debt solely based
   on age. Only exclude a balance if Q&A commentary directly
   confirms it is uncollectible. If aged balances exist but no
   Q&A commentary addresses them, flag to the director before
   proceeding and ask whether those balances should be excluded.

4. Q&A tab — review all responses for working capital relevant
   context. Pay particular attention to:
   - Invoicing and payment terms — if the company invoices and
     collects after work is performed but AR does not exist on
     the balance sheet, flag this as a bookkeeping gap
   - Any responses that reveal operational practices not
     reflected on the balance sheet
   - Any context relevant to specific component narratives below
   - Any responses confirming specific AR balances as
     uncollectible, which should be excluded from the AR
     figure per the AR Aging Review step above

If key tabs appear unpopulated or the data does not make sense,
flag this to the director before proceeding.

---

## FIRM POLICIES
- Credit card balances are excluded from working capital and
  treated as a financing item. If balances are unusually large
  relative to business size, flag to the director as a potential
  disclosure item.
- Do not calculate DPO. AP is discussed conceptually given the
  cash basis nature of most financials reviewed.
- WIP is included in the working capital figure using the most
  recent available balance, not an average. Note volatility
  across the review period and disclose that the closing balance
  may differ and a true-up is recommended.
- Do not reference whether the transaction is an asset sale or
  stock sale. Keep all language generic. The director can add
  transaction-specific language if needed.
- Only include components that are present and material, or that
  should be present given the business's operations. Do not
  include a section for an item that does not exist and is not
  relevant to the business type.

---

## SECTION ONE — INTRODUCTION
One to two sentences only.

Standard language:
"In our working capital analysis for [Company], we have
identified [X] components for the client to consider when
evaluating the working capital needs of the business."

---

## SECTION TWO — WORKING CAPITAL COMPONENTS
One concise paragraph per component. Use plain language — many
clients are first-time buyers. Briefly explain concepts that may
be unfamiliar without being condescending. Keep paragraphs tight
and operationally focused. Consolidate where possible — do not
create separate line items for AR, AR retentions, AR other, etc.
unless the distinction is operationally material and requires
separate discussion.

### COMPONENT HEADER FORMAT
Each component must be formatted as "[number]. [Component Name] -
[paragraph text continues on the same line]". For example:
"1. Cash Reserves - Maintaining adequate cash reserves is an
important consideration for any acquisition..."
The number, label, dash, and paragraph content all appear on the
same line. Do not use a standalone header with the paragraph
below. The dash in this header context is an approved exception
to the no-dash rule in narrative prose.

### COMPONENT ORDER
Current Assets first, then Current Liabilities, then Omitted
Items if applicable:

Current Assets:
1. Cash Reserves
2. Accounts Receivable
3. Inventory
4. Work in Progress / Net Billings (if applicable)
5. Prepaid and Accrued Items (combined unless a specific
   item is material)

Current Liabilities:
6. Accounts Payable
7. Sales Tax Payable (if applicable)
8. Deferred Revenue / Customer Deposits (if applicable)
9. Gift Cards (if applicable)
10. Other material current liabilities

Omitted Items:
11+. Any item relevant to the business that does not appear
     on the balance sheet

## COMPONENT STANDARD LANGUAGE

### CASH RESERVES
"Maintaining adequate cash reserves is an important
consideration for any acquisition. Based on the trailing
twelve months of normalized operating expenses, the company
averages approximately $[X] in monthly expenses, which
represents our baseline recommendation for a minimum cash
reserve. [If higher volatility: Given the [project-based /
seasonal / trades] nature of the business and the variability
in [AR collections / revenue timing], we would recommend the
client consider maintaining closer to [two months / $X] in
available cash to provide adequate cushion against timing
fluctuations.] [If lower volatility: Given the recurring and
predictable nature of the company's revenue, one month of
expenses is likely a sufficient reserve for a client with
access to additional liquidity if needed.]"

---

### ACCOUNTS RECEIVABLE
"The company's accounts receivable balance averaged $[12M avg]
over the trailing twelve months. [If consistent: The balance
was relatively consistent throughout the review period, ranging
within approximately [X]% of the average.] [If volatile: The
balance showed meaningful variability during the review period,
ranging from a low of $[X] in [month] to a high of $[X] in
[month], which is consistent with the [seasonal / project-based]
nature of the business (Q[X]).] [If AR aging report is present
and confirmed bad debt exists: Per the AR aging schedule, the
company carries approximately $[X] in aged receivables that have
been confirmed as uncollectible (Q[X]). This amount has been
excluded from the working capital estimate, and the figures
presented here reflect only the collectible AR balance of
approximately $[X]. The client should confirm with the company
how these aged balances will be treated at closing.] On average,
customers take approximately [DSO] days to pay the company after
being invoiced (Days Sales Outstanding). [If invoicing terms or
anomalies noted in Q&A: Per the company's invoicing process,
[relevant context from Q&A] (Q[X]).] [If customer concentration
exists: The client should be aware that a meaningful portion of
the AR balance is concentrated with [customer / customer type],
which introduces collection risk if that relationship changes
post-close.] The treatment of accounts receivable at closing,
whether it transfers with the business or is retained, will
affect the client's immediate working capital needs and should
be clarified in the purchase agreement."

---

### INVENTORY
[If inventory exists and is actively updated:]
"The company carried an average inventory balance of
approximately $[X] over the trailing twelve months [or: as of
[date], the most recent period for which inventory was updated].
[If consistent: Inventory levels were relatively stable
throughout the review period.] [If volatile: Inventory levels
fluctuated during the review period, which is consistent with
the [seasonal purchasing pattern / bulk ordering practice]
described by the company (Q[X]).] The client should confirm
that the inventory on hand at closing is sufficient to support
upcoming customer orders and demand. A physical inspection of
inventory prior to close is advisable to confirm the existence
and condition of the stock."

[If inventory balance has not changed over the review period:]
"The company's inventory balance has remained unchanged at $[X]
throughout the review period, suggesting it has not been actively
reconciled. [Pull any Q&A context on inventory counting process.]
The client should confirm the current state of inventory prior
to close and consider a physical count to verify the balance is
accurate and reflective of usable stock."

[If no inventory but business type suggests it should exist:]
"Based on the nature of the company's operations, the client
should discuss with the company whether any inventory or
materials on hand exist that are not reflected on the balance
sheet. This is common in businesses that expense materials as
purchased rather than carrying them as an asset, and the client
should confirm what, if anything, will be transferred at close."

---

### WORK IN PROGRESS (NET BILLINGS)
Include only for project-based businesses with longer-term
contracts (construction, HVAC, engineering, etc.).

Use the most recent available balance rather than an average.
Note volatility across the review period to give context for
why the closing balance may differ.

"Work in progress (WIP) represents the net difference between
what the company has billed to customers and what it has
actually earned based on the percentage of each project
completed. When the company has billed more than it has earned,
the net position is a liability, representing work paid for
but not yet performed. When it has earned more than it has
billed, the net position is an asset, representing work
completed but not yet invoiced. As of [most recent date], the
company's net WIP position was $[X], reflecting a net
[underbilled / overbilled] position. [If volatile: This balance
has fluctuated meaningfully over the review period, ranging
from $[X] to $[X], which is typical for a business with
variable project timing.] This figure is included in the
working capital estimate; however, the client should be aware
that the actual balance at closing may differ from the figure
presented here and a true-up based on the closing date project
schedule is recommended."

---

### PREPAID AND ACCRUED ITEMS
Include only if a balance exists. Combine into one paragraph
unless a specific item is large enough to warrant individual
discussion.

"The company carries [prepaid / accrued / both prepaid and
accrued] balances totaling approximately $[X]. These typically
relate to items such as insurance policies paid in advance and
expenses such as property taxes that are accrued over time and
paid periodically. Historical prepaid and accrued balances may
not directly reflect future obligations, as certain items will
reset or be renegotiated as part of the transition. These items
are generally not material to the overall working capital
picture but should be reviewed as part of the closing process."

---

### ACCOUNTS PAYABLE
"The company's accounts payable balance averaged $[X] over the
trailing twelve months. [If consistent: The balance was
relatively consistent throughout the review period.] [If
volatile: The balance showed variability during the review
period, ranging from $[X] to $[X], which may reflect [seasonal
purchasing patterns / timing of larger vendor payments].]
Accounts payable represents amounts owed to vendors for goods
and services already received. [If AP is absent but should
exist: Based on the company's operations, the client should
confirm whether vendor payment obligations exist that are not
being reflected on the balance sheet, as this is common in
businesses operating primarily on a cash basis.] The client
should understand that the treatment of accounts payable at
closing will affect near-term cash requirements and should be
addressed in the purchase agreement."

---

### SALES TAX PAYABLE
Include only if a balance exists.

"The company collects sales tax from customers on applicable
transactions and remits those amounts to the relevant taxing
authority on a periodic basis, typically the following month.
This creates a short-term working capital timing benefit, as
collected funds are held briefly before remittance. The
trailing twelve month average sales tax payable balance was
approximately $[X]. The client should confirm the company is
current on all sales tax filings and remittances prior to
close."

---

### DEFERRED REVENUE / CUSTOMER DEPOSITS
Include only if a balance exists or if the invoicing process
suggests it should.

"[Pull invoicing context from Q&A.] The company [collects
deposits / receives payment in advance] for [certain projects /
recurring services / other], which creates a deferred revenue
balance representing work paid for but not yet performed. The
trailing twelve month average balance was approximately $[X].
[If volatile: The balance fluctuated during the review period,
ranging from $[X] to $[X], consistent with the timing of larger
project starts.] The client should be aware that this balance
represents a real obligation, work owed to customers who have
already paid, and should be accounted for accordingly in the
purchase agreement. [If business collects upfront and is
growing: A business that collects payment before performing
work has a structural cash flow advantage that supports scaling,
as growth in customer demand generates cash before the
associated expenses are incurred.]"

---

### GIFT CARDS
Include only for retail, hospitality, or service businesses
where gift cards are issued.

Gift card balances carry an elevated risk of misstatement
relative to other working capital items. Some businesses
recognize gift card revenue immediately upon sale and remove
the balance from the balance sheet, even though a legal
obligation to the cardholder remains until redemption or
expiration. Flag this risk explicitly in the narrative
regardless of whether the balance appears on the balance sheet.

"The company issues gift cards to customers, which represent
a liability until redeemed. [If balance exists: The most
recent gift card liability balance was approximately $[X].]
The client should be aware that gift card balances carry an
elevated risk of misstatement relative to other working
capital items. In some cases, businesses recognize gift card
revenue at the time of sale and remove the balance from the
balance sheet, even though a legal obligation to the
cardholder remains. The client should confirm the company's
gift card accounting policy and verify that the balance sheet
accurately reflects all outstanding obligations prior to
close. While actual redemption rates are typically less than
the full face value of outstanding cards, the client should
not assume any portion of the balance is permanently retained
without confirming the company's historical redemption and
breakage experience."

---

### OMITTED ITEMS
Number these as additional components following the standard
list. Use this standard opening:

"Based on our review of the company's operations and the
nature of the business, [item] does not appear on the balance
sheet. [Explain why it would be expected and what the client
should do about it.] The client should discuss this with the
company prior to close to confirm whether this balance exists
and how it will be treated in the transaction."

---

## SECTION THREE — SUMMARY
Two to four sentences covering the overall working capital
picture, followed by the math display, followed by the
standard disclosure.

### MATH DISPLAY
Always show the working capital calculation in the following
format. Always include the recommended cash reserve as the
final line item in the formula so the client can see the
total liquidity need at close in one place.

When no omitted items are present:
"The working capital estimate for [Company] is as follows:
$[Cash Reserve] (Recommended Cash Reserve) + $[AR]
(Accounts Receivable) + $[Inventory] [+/- $[WIP]
(Net WIP)] - $[AP] (Accounts Payable) - $[Deferred Revenue]
(Deferred Revenue) = $[Total] in estimated working capital
and liquidity need at close."

When omitted items are present, include them in the formula
with "Amount Unknown" as the placeholder for their value.
The total is still calculated from quantified components
only, and the formula closes with "(to be adjusted for
omitted items)":
"The working capital estimate for [Company] is as follows:
$[Cash Reserve] (Recommended Cash Reserve) + $[AR]
(Accounts Receivable) + Amount Unknown ([Omitted Item Name])
- $[AP] (Accounts Payable) = $[Total] in estimated working
capital and liquidity need at close (to be adjusted for
omitted items)."

Adjust the formula to include only the components relevant
to the engagement. The formula should follow the same order
as the components discussed in Section Two: cash reserve
first, then remaining current assets in the order presented,
then current liabilities, then omitted items. The cash
reserve figure should match the recommendation made in the
Cash Reserves section (one month of expenses as a baseline,
or a higher amount if the business warrants it).

### DIRECTIONAL GUIDANCE FOR SUMMARY
Address the following where relevant:
- If working capital is positive: note that components not
  transferring at closing will need to be funded by the
  client from available cash or acquisition proceeds
- If working capital is negative: note that it may be
  advantageous for the client to structure the transaction
  so that working capital is not included, as the business
  is effectively being funded by its customers or vendors
- If the business collects before it delivers: note the
  cash flow scaling advantage
- If the business collects after it delivers: note that
  growth will require additional working capital funding

### STANDARD CLOSING DISCLOSURE
Always include verbatim:
"Working capital is inherently judgmental and will vary
based on business performance, seasonality, and the timing
and structure of the transaction. The client should ensure
they have sufficient liquidity reserves and, where
appropriate, access to a line of credit to manage any
volatility in the period following close. The figures
presented here represent a risk-adjusted estimate based on
historical performance and are intended as a starting point
for negotiation rather than a precise closing requirement."

---

## NONRELIANCE LANGUAGE
This is a quality of earnings engagement conducted under a nonreliance letter. Never phrase the narrative, the summary, or any chat commentary around it as advising the client or director to "rely on" this workbook, its figures, or its analysis. Where a figure is well supported, describe it as such (for example, "supported by the Working Capital tab") without characterizing it as something the client or director may rely upon. Where independent verification is warranted, direct the client to confirm with the company rather than to rely on the figures presented here.

## FILE-SPECIFIC RULES
- Credit card balances excluded from working capital per
  firm policy
- WIP included at most recent balance, not average,
  with closing true-up disclosure
- No DPO calculations
- Do not reference transaction structure — keep all
  language generic
- Only include components that exist or should exist
  given the business type
- Consolidate related items — do not create multiple
  line items for the same concept
- Do not use dashes in narrative output. Use
  parentheses, commas, or restructured sentences
  instead. This applies to all prose and to the
  math display formula — subtracted items should
  be expressed as "less $[X]" or restructured, not
  preceded by a dash or minus sign character
- The cash reserve figure in the math display must
  match the recommendation made in the Cash Reserves
  section exactly. If the narrative recommends two
  months of expenses, the math display must use two
  months. If it recommends one month, the math display
  must use one month. Never let the narrative
  recommendation and the math display figure differ
  without an explicit explanation in the narrative
  of why a different figure is used in the formula
- The full working capital narrative must be output
  as plain text inside a single fenced code block
  (using triple backticks with no language tag) so
  that the director can copy and paste it without
  any formatting characters, special symbols, or
  markdown rendering artifacts being carried into
  the clipboard. Do not use bold, italics, headers,
  or any other markdown formatting inside the code
  block. Numbers must appear as plain ASCII text
  (e.g., $12K, $1.5M) with no special encoding.
